## Block Grouping transfer diagnosis

All four targets were genuine policy-success/historical-narrow-MCTS-failure
instances. Both same-build rules still scored 0/4. For the two clean first
ties, policy-prior tie-breaking did exactly what was intended:

- `instance100_20`: it matched the pure policy at decision 410 and delayed the
  first divergence by 81 actions; a later unique visit maximum diverged.
- `instance100_25`: it matched the pure policy at decision 469 and delayed the
  first divergence by 177 actions; a later unique visit maximum diverged.

Thus policy-prior tie-breaking worked locally but was insufficient: later
non-tied search decisions still drove both trajectories to the 10,000-action
cap. This does not justify a broader fixed-search Block Grouping campaign.

The PW70 failure mechanism is separate. Seventeen of 27 PW failures were on
policy-success instances, and all 17 consumed the six-hour instance budget.
The leading descriptive explanation is PW70's expensive 70-simulation search
tail in Block Grouping, not a proven tie mechanism. A small traced sample would
be required before transferring the tie-break intervention to PW.

## TPP catastrophic first-update diagnosis

Phase A is complete and reproduced the historical contrast exactly: the
catastrophic seed went from a 20/20 Stage-1 source to 10/20 after one Stage-2
epoch, while the matched stable control remained 20/20.

The bad update had 43% higher mean per-step policy KL, 75% higher maximum KL,
and 21.5% more cumulative parameter movement. Both arms lost the same worker
slot and used the same replay ingestion order, so worker identity/count and
completion order do not explain the contrast.

The strongest mechanism found in code and traces is that the current policy
inside the KL term was evaluated with training-mode dropout while the frozen
anchor was evaluated deterministically. At the identical initial weights, the
reported KL was therefore already nonzero. At the first optimizer step the
anchor gradient aligned with, rather than opposed, the replay-policy gradient.
Coefficient 3 was only a soft mean penalty; it did not impose a hard maximum
on local/test-critical policy movement. Seed-specific parameter confidence and
replay-state coverage can therefore make one seed cross a destructive basin
while other 20/20 seeds remain stable.

This is a plausible mechanism, not yet a causal conclusion. Phase B now tests
checkpoint susceptibility versus replay content with only the two missing
crossovers: bad checkpoint × stable replay and stable checkpoint × bad replay.
It reuses the exact frozen 60-batch schedules; no MCTS target generation and no
100-epoch retraining are repeated. All 120 files were checksum-frozen and
verified. Smoke `21260859` gates training `21260860[0-1]`, which gates endpoint
jobs `21260862[0-1]`. No further user decision is needed.
